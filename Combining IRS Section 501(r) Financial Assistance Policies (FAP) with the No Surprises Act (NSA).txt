Keystone Health Forensic Navigator: IRS Section 501(r) Charity Care & No Surprises Act Intersect Guide
This guide establishes the legal and procedural intersection between IRS Section 501(r) Financial Assistance Policies (FAPs) and the Federal No Surprises Act (NSA), providing Pennsylvania healthcare consumers with advanced debt defense strategies against non-profit hospital billing and premature debt collection.
1. Statutory Architecture of IRS Section 501(r)
Under 26 U.S.C. § 501(r), non-profit healthcare organizations operating under 501(c)(3) tax-exempt status must comply with rigorous federal mandates regarding billing, collections, and financial assistance. Failure to adhere to these statutory rules jeopardizes the hospital’s tax-exempt status and exposes collection entities to federal and state liability.
Key Statutory Requirements
 * Financial Assistance Policy (FAP): Non-profit hospitals must maintain a widely publicized written FAP that details eligibility criteria (such as gross family income percentages relative to Federal Poverty Guidelines), free or discounted care terms, and application procedures.
 * Limitation on Charges (AGP): Under 26 U.S.C. § 501(r)(5), non-profit hospitals cannot charge patients eligible for financial assistance more than the Amounts Generally Billed (AGB) to individuals who have insurance coverage for emergency or other medically necessary care.
 * Extraordinary Collection Action (ECA) Restrictions: Under 26 U.S.C. § 501(r)(6), tax-exempt hospitals are strictly barred from initiating ECAs against a patient without first making reasonable efforts to determine FAP eligibility.
2. Mandatory Timelines & Protection Windows
Federal regulations enforce strict temporal boundaries before a non-profit hospital or its contracted collection agency can escalate a debt:
 * The 120-Day ECA Prohibition Window: Hospitals cannot initiate any ECAs (such as reporting accounts to credit bureaus, placing liens, attaching bank accounts, garnishing wages, or filing civil lawsuits) for at least 120 days following the date of the first post-discharge billing statement.
 * The 240-Day FAP Application Window: Non-profit facilities are legally required to accept and process completed FAP applications for a minimum of 240 days from the date of the initial post-discharge billing statement.
 * Retroactive Resolution of ECAs: If a patient submits a complete FAP application during the 240-day window and is determined to be eligible, the hospital must reverse any active ECAs, refund excess payments, and instruct credit reporting agencies to delete adverse trade lines.
3. Intersection with the No Surprises Act (NSA) & Pennsylvania Protections
Combining IRS Section 501(r) requirements with the Federal No Surprises Act (45 C.F.R. Part 149) and Pennsylvania’s Fair Credit Extension Uniformity Act (FCEUA) creates a multi-layered defense model:
 * Substantive Invalidation: The No Surprises Act invalidates out-of-network balance bills for emergency services and out-of-network ancillary care (e.g., anesthesiology, radiology, pathology) rendered at in-network facilities unless a valid, timely (72-hour advance) notice and consent waiver was executed.
 * Procedural Stoppage (FCEUA & FDCPA): Issuing a formal statutory dispute under 73 P.S. § 2270.4 and 15 U.S.C. § 1692g obligates the hospital's billing department and collection agents to impose an immediate administrative hold, freezing active collection efforts and prohibiting negative credit reporting while the dispute is investigated.
 * Tax-Exempt Compliance Leverage: If a non-profit hospital initiates a civil lawsuit or credit reporting within the 120-day window or before evaluating a pending FAP application, they violate Section 501(r)(6). This procedural default exposes the institution to IRS audit scrutiny and creates independent statutory claims under Pennsylvania’s Unfair Trade Practices and Consumer Protection Law (UTPCPL, 73 P.S. § 201-1 et seq.) via FCEUA incorporation.
4. Actionable Steps for Pennsylvania Consumers
 * Request the FAP & Application Immediately: Demand the hospital’s written Financial Assistance Policy, plain-language summary, and FAP application form via Certified Mail with Return Receipt Requested.
 * Audit the 120-Day Window: Verify whether any collection agency or legal counsel initiated an Extraordinary Collection Action prior to 120 days post-discharge.
 * Assert Dual-Track Non-Compliance: Combine your notice of No Surprises Act violations with an active FAP application notice to compel the billing department to halt collection, withdraw balance charges, and re-route reimbursement disputes to the Federal IDR portal.
