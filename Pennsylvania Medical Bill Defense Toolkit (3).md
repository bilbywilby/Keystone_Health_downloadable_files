# PENNSYLVANIA MEDICAL BILL DEFENSE TOOLKIT & LEGAL REFERENCE
**Action Guide and Statutory Framework for Pennsylvania Consumer Advocacy**

---

## ⚠️ CRITICAL WARNINGS & PROCEDURAL SAFEGUARDS

- **NEVER IGNORE COURT SUMMONS OR COMPLAINTS:** Expiration of Pennsylvania's $4$-year statute of limitations ($\text{42 Pa.C.S. § 5525(a)}$) does **not** automatically dismiss a lawsuit. Under **Pa.R.C.P. 1030**, the statute of limitations is an **Affirmative Defense** that must be explicitly raised under "New Matter" in a formal Answer. Failure to plead it constitutes a permanent waiver of the defense.
- **CAUTION ON PARTIAL PAYMENTS & ACKNOWLEDGMENTS:** A partial payment on an old debt can restart the $4$-year limitations period, so get legal advice before paying anything on a debt older than four years. Under Pennsylvania's common-law Acknowledgment Doctrine, a payment restarts the period only if it is a clear, unequivocal acknowledgment of the specific debt, from which a promise to pay can be inferred. No Pennsylvania statute sets a bright-line rule, so whether a particular payment qualifies depends on the specific facts (*Huntingdon Fin. Corp. v. Int'l Packaging Corp.*, 659 A.2d 1054 (Pa. Super. 1995)). Before paying, signing a payment plan, or writing anything that acknowledges the debt, consult legal aid or an attorney.
- **DISTINGUISH LIVE DEBTS FROM TIME-BARRED DEBTS:** During an active dispute, the general advice is not to pay unless counsel advises otherwise. Non-payment has real risks: a live, enforceable debt can go to collections, be reported to credit bureaus, or result in a lawsuit. Withholding payment is a strategic approach for disputed, noncompliant, or time-barred claims—not a blanket rule for every bill.
- **PRESERVE INSURANCE APPEAL TIMELINES:** Internal ERISA/ACA appeals must be filed within $180$ days of receiving an adverse benefit determination (EOB). Request a Pennsylvania Insurance Department (PID) external review within four months of the insurer's final adverse benefit determination.

## PART 1: THE TWO-TRACK DISPUTE ARCHITECTURE

When contesting unlawful medical debt or out-of-network balance bills, consumers must execute a dual-track strategy to separate coverage adjudication from collection enforcement:

```
                      STRATEGIC TWO-TRACK DISPUTE ARCHITECTURE

                 [ Unlawful Out-of-Network / Disputed Medical Bill ]
                                        │
        ┌───────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┐
        ▼                                                               ▼
 [ TRACK 1: INSURER ADJUDICATION ]                         [ TRACK 2: PROVIDER RESCISSION ]
        │                                                               │
        ▼                                                               ▼
 Submit Formal Internal Appeal                              Issue Written Notice of Dispute
 to Health Plan (within 180 Days)                           Citing 45 C.F.R. § 149 & FCEUA
        │                                                               │
        ├───────────────────────────────┐                               ▼
        ▼                               ▼                   Demand Immediate Hold &
 [ Appeal Approved ]            [ Appeal Denied ]           Account Rescission
 (Claim Paid at OON Rate)       (In-Network Rate Kept)                  │
                                        │                               ▼
                                        ▼                   [ Collection Activity Paused ]
                                Escalate to PID                         │
                                External Review                         ▼
                         (Request within 4 Months)
```

1. **Track 1: Insurer Coverage Adjudication**
   - Targets the health plan's claim calculation and cost-sharing determination.
   - If a commercial health plan miscalculates cost-sharing or denies No Surprises Act protections, submit an internal appeal within $180$ days. If denied, request a PID Independent External Review within four months of the final adverse determination.
   - *Note on Plan Types:* PID external review applies to state-regulated, fully insured plans. Self-funded ERISA plans use the federal external review process instead.

2. **Track 2: Provider & Collection Rescission**
   - Targets the billing department, hospital, or third-party debt collector.
   - Issues a formal Notice of Statutory Dispute invoking the No Surprises Act ($\text{45 C.F.R. Part 149}$), IRS Section $501(\text{r})$ rules ($\text{Treas. Reg. § 1.501(r)-6}$), the PA Fair Credit Extension Uniformity Act ($\text{73 P.S. § 2270.4}$), and PA contract limitations ($\text{42 Pa.C.S. § 5525(a)}$) to freeze collection activity.

## PART 2: CONSUMER ACTION CHECKLIST

### Step 1: Triage Your Bill ($5$ Minutes)
- **Emergency or Out-of-Network Ancillary Care?** (Anesthesia, radiology, pathology at an in-network facility) $\rightarrow$ Protected under the No Surprises Act ($\text{45 C.F.R. § 149.110}$ / § $149.120$). Capped at in-network Qualifying Payment Amount (QPA) cost-sharing.
- **Non-Profit Tax-Exempt Hospital?** $\rightarrow$ Protected under Treasury Regulation $\text{§ 1.501(r)-6}$ ($120$-day ECA prohibition window; $240$-day FAP application window).
- **Debt Age $> 4$ Years from Default?** $\rightarrow$ Time-barred under $\text{42 Pa.C.S. § 5525(a)}$.

### Step 2: Assemble Evidence (Day $1$)
- [ ] Itemized billing statement from provider
- [ ] Explanation of Benefits (EOB) from insurer
- [ ] Copies of insurance cards (front and back)
- [ ] Intake documents and notice/consent waivers (if any)
- [ ] Audit log documenting initial statement date and actual default date (`accrual_date`)

### Step 3: Serve Written Statutory Dispute & Debt Validation (Days $1 \text{--} 30$)
- Send via **USPS Certified Mail with Return Receipt Requested**.
- Include written debt validation request under $\text{15 U.S.C. § 1692g}$ (within $30$ days of first collection contact for third-party collectors).
- Include explicit non-acknowledgment phrasing to avoid restarting limitation clocks under the Acknowledgment Doctrine.

### Step 4: Administrative Regulatory Escalation (Days $30 \text{--} 60$)
If collectors fail to verify or continue collection during an active dispute, file concurrent complaints with:
- **PA Insurance Department (PID):** $1\text{-}877\text{-}881\text{-}6388$ ($\text{insurance.pa.gov}$) for state-regulated plans.
- **CMS No Surprises Help Desk:** $1\text{-}800\text{-}985\text{-}3059$ ($\text{cms.gov/nosurprises}$) for self-funded ERISA or federal issues.
- **PA Attorney General (Bureau of Consumer Protection):** $1\text{-}800\text{-}441\text{-}2555$ ($\text{attorneygeneral.gov}$) for FCEUA / UTPCPL debt collection violations. Note: Two-year deadline applies to file FCEUA claims from the date of violation.

### Template A: Notice of Statutory Dispute & Balance Billing Invalidation
```text
[Your Full Name]
[Street Address]

[Phone] | [Email]

[Date]

VIA CERTIFIED MAIL, RETURN RECEIPT REQUESTED
Tracking #: [Number]

[City, State ZIP]

Re: Formal Notice of Statutory Dispute & Demand for Verification / Rescission
    Account / Reference #: [Account Number]
    Date(s) of Service: [Dates]
    Disputed Balance: $[Amount]

Dear Billing Manager / Collections Supervisor:

I am writing to formally dispute the balance referenced above. Please treat this communication as a formal written dispute and demand for verification.

1. Federal No Surprises Act Protections (45 C.F.R. Part 149)
The services rendered on [Date] constituted [emergency services / out-of-network ancillary services (anesthesiology, radiology, pathology, assistant surgery) performed at an in-network facility]. Under 45 C.F.R. § 149.110 and § 149.120, out-of-network providers are prohibited from balance billing patients beyond established in-network cost-sharing based on the Qualifying Payment Amount (QPA). No valid, timely notice-and-consent waiver complying with 45 C.F.R. § 149.420 was executed. Pursuant to 45 C.F.R. § 149.510, reimbursement disputes must be resolved directly between your entity and my health plan via Federal Independent Dispute Resolution (IDR).

2. Pennsylvania Fair Credit Extension Uniformity Act (73 P.S. § 2270.4)
Pursuant to 73 P.S. § 2270.4, attempting to collect amounts that are not legally owed or pursuing collection activity while an account is formally disputed constitutes an unfair debt collection practice. Under 73 P.S. § 2270.5(a), any violation of the FCEUA constitutes a per se violation of the Pennsylvania Unfair Trade Practices and Consumer Protection Law (UTPCPL), 73 P.S. § 201-1 et seq., exposing non-compliant entities to private statutory remedies, treble damages, and attorney's fees.

3. Non-Acknowledgment of Debt
Nothing in this letter admits liability, acknowledges the debt, or promises payment. I dispute this balance and am not making any payment or payment arrangement while the dispute is pending. This letter is not an acknowledgment of the debt for any purpose.

4. Demand for Remedial Action
Please take the following actions within thirty (30) days:
a. Freeze all active collection efforts, telephonic demands, and credit bureau reporting.
b. Rescind the out-of-network balance charge, adjusting patient responsibility to $0.00 (or the verified in-network cost-sharing level).
c. Provide written confirmation that this account has been adjusted or returned to the primary creditor.

Sincerely,

[Signature]
[Printed Name]
```

## PART 4: STATUTORY REFERENCE & CASE LAW MATRIX

| Authority / Citation | Legal Scope & Rule | Consumer Defense & Litigation Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **No Surprises Act** ($\text{45 C.F.R. Part 149}$) | Caps patient liability for emergency & ancillary care at in-network QPA rates. | Removes patient from payer-provider payment disputes; mandates Federal IDR. |
| **IRS Section 501(r)** ($\text{Treas. Reg. § 1.501(r)-6}$, *counsel to confirm*) | Non-profit hospitals must wait $\ge 120$ days post-statement before ECAs and allow $\ge 240$ days for FAP applications. | No private right of action for consumers; use to request written FAP screening and pause collection. |
| **PA Contract Limitations** ($\text{42 Pa.C.S. § 5525(a)}$) | $4$-year statute of limitations on written/unwritten contracts generally running from default. | Absolute defense against untimely suits; **must be pleaded under Pa.R.C.P. 1030 (New Matter)**. |
| **PA Acknowledgment Doctrine** (Common Law) | Payment or written promise restarts the limitations period *only* if it is a clear, unequivocal acknowledgment. | Fact-dependent. Disputing or requesting itemization does not restart the clock. |
| **Federal Regulation F** ($\text{12 C.F.R. § 1006.26}$) | Prohibits debt collectors from suing or threatening to sue on time-barred debt. | Applies strictly to third-party collectors. |
| **Federal FDCPA Validation** ($\text{15 U.S.C. § 1692g}$) | Requires collector to halt collection after timely written dispute until verification is mailed. | Applies to third-party collectors, not hospitals collecting under their own name. |
| **FCRA Tradeline Disputes** ($\text{15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(a)(3)}$ / $\text{(b)}$) | Mandates reporting debt as disputed and conducting investigation upon notification. | File disputes directly with both credit bureaus and furnisher. |
| **PA FCEUA** ($\text{73 P.S. § 2270.4}$ et seq.) | Covers original creditors (hospitals) and third-party collectors against unfair practices. | Violations trigger *per se* UTPCPL remedies under $\text{73 P.S. § 2270.5(a)}$; 2-year limitation to sue. |
| **PA Wage Exemption** ($\text{42 Pa.C.S. § 8127}$) | Broadly protects wages in employer hands from attachment for ordinary debt. | Does not protect bank accounts or liquid assets once deposited. |
| **Tenancy by the Entireties** | Shields jointly owned marital property from individual debts of one spouse. | Does not protect joint debts; federal tax debts are an exception. |
