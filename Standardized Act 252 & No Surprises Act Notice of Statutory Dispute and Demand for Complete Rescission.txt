I. INTRODUCTION AND STATEMENT OF DISPUTE
Please take notice that the undersigned consumer formally and strictly disputes the balance billing statement dated [Insert Statement Date] in the amount of $[Insert Disputed Amount], issued for medical services rendered on [Insert Date of Service] at [Insert In-Network Facility Name].
This billing statement constitutes an illegal out-of-network balance bill in direct violation of the federal No Surprises Act (NSA) (Title I of Division BB of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2021; 45 C.F.R. Part 149), Pennsylvania Act 252 (2023), and Pennsylvania consumer protection statutes.
II. LEGAL GROUNDS FOR INVALIDATION AND RESCISSION
 * Violation of Federal No Surprises Act Protections (45 C.F.R. Part 149):
   The services in question involved [choose applicable: emergency care / air ambulance transport / ancillary clinical services, including anesthesiology, radiology, pathology, neonatology, or assistant surgery] performed at an in-network facility. Under 45 C.F.R. § 149.30 et seq., out-of-network providers are strictly prohibited from billing patients beyond the established in-network cost-sharing amount, calculated using the Qualifying Payment Amount (QPA). No valid, executed notice-and-consent waiver conforming to the strict statutory and temporal requirements of 45 C.F.R. § 149.420 was ever provided or executed prior to the delivery of services.
 * Violation of Pennsylvania Act 252 Point-of-Service Disclosure Mandates:
   Under Pennsylvania Act 252 (2023), healthcare providers are legally mandated to deliver real-time written or electronic disclosure of their network status at the point of service prior to rendering non-emergency care. The provider failed to furnish this mandatory disclosure during intake or scheduling. Consequently, the resulting balance bill constitutes an immediate statutory violation under both Act 252 and federal law.
 * Interference with Federal IDR Gateway (45 C.F.R. § 149.510):
   Under federal law, payment disputes regarding out-of-network reimbursement must be resolved exclusively between the health plan and the provider through the Federal Independent Dispute Resolution (IDR) portal. Passing uncollected reimbursement deficits onto the insured patient is unlawful.
III. DEMANDS FOR IMMEDIATE REMEDIAL ACTION
In light of the foregoing statutory violations, the undersigned demands that you take the following actions immediately:
 * Complete Rescission of Balance Bill: Immediately cancel and rescind the out-of-network balance charge of $[Insert Disputed Amount], reducing the patient's personal financial liability to $0.00 (or the verified in-network cost-sharing amount determined by the insurer).
 * Cease Collection and Credit Reporting: In accordance with the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA) (15 U.S.C. § 1692 et seq.) and the Pennsylvania Fair Credit Extension Uniformity Act (FCEUA) (73 P.S. § 2270.4), immediately freeze all collection activities, halt telephone and written demands, and ensure no negative trade lines or derogatory data are transmitted to any consumer reporting agencies.
 * Redirection to Federal IDR: Withdraw all direct consumer billing and pursue any reimbursement claims directly against the health insurance carrier via the Federal IDR portal pursuant to 45 C.F.R. § 149.510.
IV. RESERVATION OF LEGAL RIGHTS AND STATUTORY REMEDIES
Be advised that pursuing an unlawful balance bill while a formal statutory dispute is active constitutes an unfair, deceptive, and unconscionable collection practice under the Pennsylvania Fair Credit Extension Uniformity Act (FCEUA) (73 P.S. § 2270.4). Because violations of the FCEUA constitute per se violations of the Pennsylvania Unfair Trade Practices and Consumer Protection Law (UTPCPL) (73 P.S. § 201-1 et seq.), continuing collection efforts exposes your institution and billing agents to private civil lawsuits, actual damages, treble statutory damages, and mandatory attorney fee awards (73 P.S. § 201-9.2).
Furthermore, pursuant to 42 Pa.C.S. § 5525(a), the statutory limitation period for medical contract claims is strictly four years from the date of default. Notice is hereby given that the patient refuses to make any partial payments or token disbursements, as any voluntary payment or written debt acknowledgment would unlawfully reset the four-year statute of limitations clock under Pennsylvania law.
Please provide written confirmation within thirty (30) days of receipt of this notice that the disputed balance has been fully rescinded and that all collection actions have been permanently terminated.
